One-path inference for long-run portfolio growth in stochastic factor markets

Xinrui Dong · Working paper, 2026

Studying uncertainty in long-run portfolio growth from one observed wealth trajectory. Numerical experiments and a descriptive U.S. equity application examine variance estimation, finite-sample behavior, and the effects of wealth construction.

Read abstract

Assessing long-run portfolio rankings requires estimating uncertainty from a finite wealth record. We study this uncertainty for fixed feedback portfolios with shared factor and asset noise in an Itô diffusion market. Under dissipativity, uniform ellipticity, polynomial drift growth and local Hölder regularity, a Poisson corrector separates portfolio noise, factor-transmitted noise and their interaction in long-run variance. An explicit semigroup expression identifies the leading finite-block bias with the same noise structure. A standard batch-means statistic consistently estimates the variance from sampled wealth values, without estimating factor dynamics or the corrector. This yields feasible inference for growth rates and fixed-strategy differences. The Kelly rule provides an explicit benchmark. In a linear-Gaussian market, varying factor-asset correlation changes long-run variance by a factor of 2.3 at the illustrative calibration, while leaving growth unchanged. The weak-signal detection horizon scales as the inverse square of the predictive slope. Simulations quantify variance estimation, grid effects and finite-horizon coverage. A descriptive U.S. equity application compares diffusion-based wealth reconstruction with monthly self-financing accounting and shows how wealth construction affects growth comparisons.
